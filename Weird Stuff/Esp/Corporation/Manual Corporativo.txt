1. PURPOSE
   ============================================================

This Manual establishes the general internal framework governing
Weird Stuff, including its organizational structure, authority,
internal responsibilities, product management, development,
quality assurance, security, access control, communications,
and general operational standards.

This Manual is the primary internal corporate reference for
the matters covered by it.

This Manual does not replace applicable law, contracts, or
specific legal documents.

[MANDATORY]
All members, collaborators, and other persons acting on behalf
of Weird Stuff must comply with the mandatory provisions of
this Manual within the scope of their responsibilities.

[MANDATORY]
Any specific internal policy or procedure adopted by Weird Stuff
must be compatible with this Manual.

============================================================

3. NORMATIVE CLASSIFICATION
===========================

The following classifications are used throughout this Manual.

[MANDATORY]
A MANDATORY rule is an internal requirement that must be followed.

[PROCEDURE]
A PROCEDURE defines how a requirement should be carried out.

[STANDARD]
A STANDARD establishes an adopted technical or operational
requirement.

[RECOMMENDED]
A RECOMMENDED provision describes a preferred practice that is
not mandatory solely because it is marked as recommended.

[PENDING]
A PENDING item identifies a matter that has not yet been
formally defined.

[RESERVED]
A RESERVED item contains information that is intentionally
restricted from publication.

5. CORPORATE AUTHORITY
======================

Weird Stuff operates under the following internal organizational
structure:

CEO
|
+-- Director
|
+-- Manager
|
+-- Specialized Teams

5.1 CEO

The CEO is the highest internal executive authority of Weird Stuff,
subject to any legal limitations that may apply.

[MANDATORY]
The CEO may:

* Establish general organizational direction.
* Approve internal policies.
* Assign responsibilities.
* Create, remove, or reorganize teams.
* Approve major projects and initiatives.
* Resolve authority disputes.
* Delegate responsibilities.
* Take urgent measures when necessary.

[MANDATORY]
Delegation of a responsibility does not automatically remove the
need for appropriate oversight.

5.2 DIRECTOR

The Director is the highest operational authority below the CEO.

[MANDATORY]
The Director is responsible for:

* Coordinating general operations.
* Supervising assigned managers and teams.
* Implementing approved decisions.
* Escalating significant issues.
* Coordinating work between departments.

5.3 MANAGER

The Manager coordinates the teams and operational activities
assigned to them.

[MANDATORY]
The Manager must monitor assigned work, communicate relevant
issues, and escalate matters exceeding their authority.

5.4 SPECIALIZED TEAMS

Weird Stuff may maintain specialized teams including:

* Development
* Design
* QA / Testing
* Product
* Infrastructure
* Security

The exact composition of teams may change according to the
needs of Weird Stuff.

7. INTERNAL CONDUCT
===================

All persons participating in Weird Stuff are expected to behave
responsibly within the scope of their role.

[MANDATORY]
Weird Stuff resources must not intentionally be used for:

* Unauthorized access.
* Credential theft.
* Fraud.
* Deliberate malware distribution.
* Impersonation.
* Unauthorized attacks against systems.
* Malicious manipulation of infrastructure.
* Illegal activities.

[MANDATORY]
Internal information classified as restricted or confidential
must not be disclosed without authorization.

[MANDATORY]
A person must not intentionally interfere with the work,
accounts, repositories, infrastructure, or property of another
member without authorization.

============================================================
8. CONFLICTS OF INTEREST
========================

[MANDATORY]
A relevant conflict of interest must be disclosed to the
appropriate responsible person.

[RECOMMENDED]
Sensitive decisions should be reviewed by an additional person
when a conflict of interest may affect the decision.

[MANDATORY]
A person with a known conflict must not conceal that conflict
when it could materially affect Weird Stuff or its projects.

============================================================
9. ACCESS CONTROL
=================

[MANDATORY]
Access to internal systems must be based on an actual operational
need.

[MANDATORY]
Permissions must be limited to what is reasonably necessary.

[MANDATORY]
Access must be removed when the associated need no longer exists.

[MANDATORY]
Personal passwords and authentication credentials must not be
shared.

[MANDATORY]
Administrative access must receive stronger protection than
ordinary user access.

[RECOMMENDED]
Multi-factor authentication should be enabled on administrative
and critical accounts whenever available.

[RECOMMENDED]
Critical access permissions should be reviewed periodically.

============================================================
10. INTERNAL INFORMATION
========================

Information may be classified as:

PUBLIC
Information intended for public release.

INTERNAL
Information intended for members of Weird Stuff.

RESTRICTED
Information accessible only to specifically authorized people.

CONFIDENTIAL
Information whose unauthorized disclosure could cause significant
harm to Weird Stuff, its members, users, or projects.

[MANDATORY]
Restricted and confidential information must only be shared with
authorized recipients.

[MANDATORY]
A person must not publish internal information simply because
they have access to it.

11. PERSONNEL AND COLLABORATORS
===============================

[MANDATORY]
Every person receiving access to internal resources must have an
identified function, responsibility, or justified operational need.

[MANDATORY]

[RECOMMENDED]
New members should receive an introduction to the tools,
responsibilities, communication channels, and applicable rules.

[MANDATORY]

13. PRODUCT LIFECYCLE
=====================

CONCEPT
->
DEVELOPMENT
->
TESTING
->
RELEASE
->
MAINTENANCE
->
RETIREMENT

[RECOMMENDED]

============================================================
14. SOFTWARE DEVELOPMENT
========================

14.1 SOURCE CODE

[MANDATORY]
Source code must be stored in authorized repositories or systems.

[MANDATORY]
Passwords, private keys, API keys, tokens, and other secrets must
not intentionally be committed to public repositories.

[MANDATORY]
Important modifications must remain traceable through version
control.

14.2 CODE REVIEW

[RECOMMENDED]
Important changes should be reviewed by another qualified person
where team size permits.

[MANDATORY]
Security-critical modifications must receive an appropriate
review before release whenever the available team structure
allows it.

14.3 DEPENDENCIES

[MANDATORY]
Important external dependencies must be known to the responsible
development team.

[MANDATORY]
Third-party software must only be used in accordance with its
applicable license.

[MANDATORY]
Software with an identified security or licensing risk that is
incompatible with the product must not knowingly be introduced.

[RECOMMENDED]
Complex products should maintain a dependency inventory.

============================================================
15. QUALITY ASSURANCE AND TESTING
=================================

[MANDATORY]
Stable releases must undergo reasonable testing appropriate to
their functionality and risk.

[MANDATORY]
Known critical defects affecting essential operation, security,
or data integrity must be evaluated before stable release.

[MANDATORY]
Important bugs must contain enough information to determine their
impact and, when possible, reproduce the issue.

[RECOMMENDED]
Major changes should receive regression testing.

[RECOMMENDED]
Important fixes should retain evidence showing that the fix was
tested.

============================================================
16. RELEASE MANAGEMENT
======================

[MANDATORY]
Every public release must have an identifiable version.

[MANDATORY]
Published versions must remain distinguishable from development
versions.

[MANDATORY]
A release must not intentionally conceal material differences from
the version it claims to be.

[RECOMMENDED]
Significant releases should contain release notes.

[MANDATORY]
Automatic updates must use reasonable mechanisms to protect the
integrity and authenticity of distributed update packages.

[RECOMMENDED]
Artifact signing and integrity verification should be used for
important products where technically possible.

============================================================
17. SECURITY
============

Security is a shared responsibility across Weird Stuff.

[MANDATORY]
Known significant vulnerabilities must be assessed and, where
appropriate, corrected.

[MANDATORY]
Credentials suspected of compromise must be revoked, rotated,
or otherwise secured as soon as reasonably possible.

[MANDATORY]
Security incidents must be reported to the appropriate responsible
person.

[MANDATORY]
Security controls must not intentionally be disabled without
authorization.

[RECOMMENDED]
Critical systems should use multi-factor authentication,
logging, access review, and backup controls where available.

============================================================
18. SECURITY INCIDENTS
======================

A security incident may include an event affecting:

* Confidentiality
* Integrity
* Availability
* Authenticity
* Security of users
* Security of Weird Stuff systems

[MANDATORY]
Incidents must be reported as soon as reasonably possible.

The general response order is:

1. Contain
2. Protect
3. Recover
4. Investigate
5. Correct
6. Prevent recurrence

[RECOMMENDED]
Significant incidents should receive a post-incident review.
